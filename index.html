The Spark

It Didn't Start
With a Plan.

It started with a quiet conversation over tea in mid-2005. Post a successful PSLV launch, the Hon'ble Prime Minister of India, Dr. Manmohan Singh, raised a serious concern with the Chairman of ISRO: Could ISRO continue to attract bright young talent in the face of a booming private sector?

That question became a mission. The Chairman turned to Dr. B.N. Suresh with a challenge: create a structured program to train the next generation of space engineers. There was no infrastructure, no syllabus, and no faculty—just a shared necessity to build something that didn't exist yet.

The idea was rare: to create a space where students don't just learn facts—they learn to think, question, and build things that outlast them. That idea, fragile at first, became our compass.

Introduction
"Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology was not born from abundance.
It was born from belief."

What began as a bold proposal in January 2007 has since grown into a living, breathing community of learners, thinkers, and builders. Founded in September 2007 in the coastal city of Thiruvananthapuram, the institute was never designed to be ordinary. It was designed to be the backbone of India's journey to the stars.

Over the years, it has evolved through struggle, breakthrough, and reinvention—shaped equally by the pioneers who walked through the doors of the VSSC Training School and the problems they arrived determined to solve. This is not just a story about an institution. This is a story about what happens when a nation refuses to settle for anything less than excellence.

The Journey

Year by Year, Step by Step

Each milestone, a chapter of its own.

Mid 2005
The Idea

A Question over tea

In mid-2005, after a successful PSLV launch, a meeting with Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh turned into an informal conversation. In that moment, a pressing question emerged, could ISRO continue to attract young talent in a changing world? From that question came a vision: to create a structured program that would train future space engineers and carry the mission forward.

January, 2007 - April, 2007
The Blueprint

The Proposal That Set It in Motion

At ISRO Headquarters, the idea began to take shape. Senior officials came together to translate a question into a concrete proposal, one that could define the future of space education in the country. In January 2007, the proposal was formally submitted by the Chairman of ISRO for approval. By April that same year, it was accepted. With that decision, the vision found its footing. The plans for the Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology were no longer an idea, they had been set in motion.

June, 2007 - September, 2007
Racing Against Time

A team had to be built, and there was no time to waste

With infrastructure and curriculum falling into place, one crucial piece was still missing, the people. In June 2007, a nationwide call was made. By August, interviews were underway. What followed was unprecedented: decisions were made instantly, and appointment letters were issued the very same day. Skepticism lingered, but the pace never slowed. By September 10, the faculty stood assembled, just 4 days before classes were set to begin!!

May, 2012
The Moment It Left the Ground

What began as an idea had now found its way to the sky.

What once lived on paper, in classrooms and labs, now stood ready on the launchpad, built by the very students the institute was created to nurture. As the countdown reached its final seconds, it was no longer just a rocket. It was the proof that the vision had worked, that the journey had meaning. And then, Vyom rose. Not just into the sky, but into the story the institute had been writing from the very beginning. The day was 11th May, 2012.

August 2010
Phase Five

Expanding the Circle: The Move to Valiamala

By 2010, the temporary corridors at VSSC were overflowing. A new mission reached its peak: the move to our permanent home. After an "18-month miracle" of construction on war footing, the Valiamala campus was ready. On August 15, 2010, as the National Flag was hoisted at the new site, Dr. B.N. Suresh described a sense of awe similar to Neil Armstrong landing on the moon. The institute had finally found its independent ground.

Now
Present

Still Writing the Story

The institute today is not a finished product—it is a living work in progress. Every student who walks through the door adds a line to the mission. From the development of complex satellite systems to pioneering research in advanced space technology, the spirit of the "120-day miracle" remains alive. We are no longer just dreaming of the stars; we are the ones actively engineering the future of space exploration. The story continues.

Story Chapters

Five Moments That
Defined Everything

I
The Beginning

A Room, An Idea, A Risk

Every origin story starts somewhere ordinary. Ours began in the Alternate Training Facility (ATF) of VSSC. In May 2007, there were no resources, no guarantees, and no precedent—only a four-month deadline to welcome the first batch. There was only the conviction that something worth building was worth the uncertainty of beginning.

II
Early Struggles

When Nothing Goes to Plan

The early years tested everything. The planned campus at Ponmudy was suddenly halted by legal and ecological hurdles, leaving the institute’s future hanging in the balance. There were months when resources ran thin and doubt ran high, but the people who stayed did so not because it was easy—they stayed because they could see what the institute was becoming.

III
Turning Point

The Day the Wind Changed

There are moments that divide a story into before and after. For us, it was the decision to carve 50 acres out of the existing ISRO land at Valiamala. Suddenly, the work that had felt invisible began to matter in rooms we had never entered. Through an "18-month miracle" of construction on war footing, the dream finally found its permanent ground.

IV
Growth Phase

Growing Without Losing Ourselves

Growth brought new people, new programs, and new pressures. The challenge was never how to grow—it was how to grow without betraying the "Tinkering Spirit" that made us worth growing in the first place. From the establishment of world-class labs to the launch of Asia’s first student sounding rocket, that tension sharpened us. We emerged from it clearer than ever.

V
Present Day

Here, Now, Still Becoming

Today, IIST stands as something we could not have fully imagined at the start. It is larger, yes—but more importantly, it is deeper. We have evolved from a fledgling experiment into a vital partner for the nation's space missions. The questions we ask are harder, the answers we pursue are more urgent, and the work, as always, is far from finished.

The Story

Chapter 01

The Room Where It Happened

On Beginnings & Belief

There was a room in mid-2005—an informal tea gathering with Prime Minister Dr. Manmohan Singh. Nothing special about it, but something happened there that no blueprint could have predicted. A question was asked about attracting talent to space, and for the first time, a vision for IIST took root. That room was the beginning of everything.

Chapter 02

We Almost Didn't Make It

On Doubt & Perseverance

May 2007 was the hardest. Resources had stretched thin, the Ponmudy land was locked in ecological legal battles, and the goal of starting a college in four months felt practically impossible. What kept us going was the memory of why we started: a firm belief that given the right direction, people can surprise themselves with what they achieve.

Chapter 03

The Student Who Changed the Question

On Purpose & People

It was a student—not a donor or an award—that made us understand why the institute matured. Whether sitting on the lawn together or ensuring the quality of the mess food, the focus was always on the human side of engineering. That was the moment we understood: we weren't just teaching answers; we were teaching how to find better questions through hands-on tinkering and satellite projects.

Chapter 04

The Year We Said No

On Identity & Integrity

Growth brought offers and pressures to compromise, but we declined. When advised to have a "Plan B" for the campus, the answer was a firm no—because a backup plan only weakens the commitment to Plan A. Every "no" was an act of definition, ensuring the institute never fell behind in knowledge or the pursuit of excellence.

Chapter 05

What We're Still Figuring Out

On the Ongoing Story

The honest truth is that we don’t have everything worked out, and that's the point. The world keeps changing, from our transition to university status in 2020 to the new frontiers of satellite research. The story started in that mismatched-chair room is still being written by students building satellites today. And the best parts, we believe, are ahead.

2010
The Road Ahead

The Story Doesn't End.
It Deepens.

What started as one restless idea has become something we could not have imagined alone. An institution is not a building or a logo — it is the accumulated courage of everyone who ever chose to be part of it.

We look back not to admire the distance we've covered, but to understand the direction we're headed. Every chapter we've lived has been preparation for the ones we haven't written yet.

If this place has taught us anything, it's that the most important work is always the work that hasn't happened yet. We remain, as always, at the beginning.
